The Slow Road

Privacy

Effective November 2, 2026

I'm Brandon, and I make The Slow Road on my own. This page says what the app does with your information, in plain words.

The short version

  • What you record stays on your phone, with a backup copy in your own iCloud if you use iCloud.
  • The app has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and I keep nothing you record.
  • A few things have to leave the phone to work: a photo you ask Identify to name goes through a small server of mine to Anthropic, map tiles come from map providers, and search, directions and the offline map come from Apple.

What stays on your phone

Everything you record is saved on your phone: the places you've marked, your field journal and its photos, your field guide, your trips and the GPS track of each drive, your saved plans, the stops you've visited, and your Home point. Your settings stay on the phone too.

The app uses your location on the phone, to record the roads you drive, show where you are on the map, and play Bill's stories when Bill is on. The app doesn't send your location to me. Three things below can carry it: the GPS tracks of your drives go into your own iCloud backup, the map tiles your phone asks for show which piece of map you're looking at, and a trip planned from where you are or from your Home can send that point, or the nearest point on a road to it, to Apple, as the Apple section says.

Photos you take with Identify's camera and save are also added to a "The Slow Road" album in your Photos library.

Your iCloud backup

If you use iCloud on your phone, the app copies your record into its own folder in your iCloud Drive, so a new phone can get it back. That copy holds everything in the list above. A drive still in progress, the app's crash-recovery copy of it, the plan you're following right now and the saved map tiles are never copied.

The copy lives in your own iCloud account and counts against your iCloud storage. Your phone does the uploading; the app doesn't send it to any server. Apple encrypts it in transit and on its servers. Apple explains how it protects iCloud data on its iCloud data security page.

Identify

When you ask Identify to name something, the app sends that one photo, with the app's own fixed instructions, to a small server I run, and the server passes it on to Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI. Before it goes, the photo is redrawn as a new image, so it carries no location, no camera make or model, and no other photo details. Nothing you've recorded goes with it: no location, no notes, nothing from your journal.

The first time a photo would go, the app asks you first, and only Allow sends it. Allow is kept on your phone; Not Now sends nothing, and the app asks again next time. To withdraw Allow, open More, then Identify, then Withdraw permission. Identify will ask again before it sends another photo. Withdrawing doesn't delete anything already sent or saved.

Identify's server

The server is there for three reasons. It holds the app's key to Anthropic, so the key never sits on a phone. It gives each copy of the app a fair share of identifications each day. And it can turn away a copy of the app that's being misused.

To do that, it keeps this about each copy of the app:

  • A key for your copy of the app. The first time you use Identify, your phone makes a key with App Attest, a part of iOS, and Apple vouches that it belongs to a genuine copy of The Slow Road. The server keeps the key's public half, its ID, and what it needs to check that each request is signed by that key. The key belongs to the app on your phone: it isn't your Apple ID, your phone's name or anything you typed.
  • A count. The server counts how many photos your copy of the app has had identified today, so it can hold each copy to its daily share, and it keeps the time your copy last used Identify. A try that doesn't come back with an identification doesn't count.

With each photo, the server sends Anthropic a tag for your copy of the app: a scrambled code made from the key's ID, never anything you entered. Anthropic may use it to help spot misuse. If a copy of the app is misused, the server can turn away every request that carries its tag. The server keeps the tags only of copies it has turned away.

When your copy of the app hasn't used Identify for 90 days, the server deletes its key, its count and its last-use time. The server keeps no photo, no answer, no IP address and no logs. Across all copies of the app it keeps only the day's total, which names no one.

The server runs on Cloudflare. Your phone's request reaches it with your phone's IP address, as any internet request does; Cloudflare carries that traffic, and Cloudflare's privacy policy covers it. My server doesn't keep it. The server makes its own request to Anthropic, which doesn't carry your phone's IP address or its request details.

What Anthropic keeps

Anthropic deletes what the app sends, the photo and the tag with it, within 30 days. If its automated safety systems flag a request, it can keep it for up to 2 years. That's from Anthropic's page on how long it stores data.

Maps

The maps are drawn from tiles, small squares of map for one place at one zoom level. Some tiles come built into the app, the offline map holds more if you download it, and tiles you've already looked at are saved on your phone so they work without a signal. When the app needs a tile it doesn't have, it asks a map provider for it. That request tells the provider which piece of map you're looking at, and it arrives with your phone's IP address and the standard details any web request carries. Here is who the providers are and what each says it keeps:

  • OpenFreeMap supplies the streets, places and labels on the road and places maps. It says it doesn't log IP addresses by default and keeps anonymous logs of things like browser type, operating system and time. Its error logs can include the IP address and what was asked for, and are deleted after 7 days, and it may log IP addresses for up to 30 days during a security incident (its privacy policy).
  • Mapterhorn supplies the terrain shading on those maps. It says it keeps the IP address and user agent of each request for up to 30 days (its privacy policy).
  • The U.S. Geological Survey's The National Map supplies the topographic maps, like the one in your field journal. USGS says it logs the IP address, browser and operating system, time and what was requested, for running its sites (its privacy policy).

Apple

  • When you search for a place in the trip planner, or for an address when you choose your Home, the words you type go to Apple's map search.
  • The trip planner's map and the map you use to choose your Home are Apple Maps.
  • When you ask the app to suggest how to split a leg of a planned trip, the start and end of that leg go to Apple. If you planned from your Home or from where you are, the start is that point, or the nearest point on a road to it.
  • When you open a leg of a trip in Apple Maps, the app gives Apple Maps the leg's end and its start. The start is either the leg's planned start, which can be your Home or where you were when you planned the trip, or your current location, which Apple Maps finds for itself.
  • When you open a place in Apple Maps from the map, that place's location and name go to Apple.
  • If you download the offline map, it comes from Apple, which hosts it for the app. The app downloads it only when you ask, after telling you its size.
  • The first time you use Identify, your phone asks Apple to vouch for the app's key, as the section on Identify's server says.

Apple's privacy policy covers what Apple does with these.

The app sends no crash reports or usage statistics of its own. If you've turned on Share With App Developers in iOS, Apple may share crash data and statistics with me, in a form that doesn't identify you. You can turn that off in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements (Apple's page).

Links and sharing

A link on a page, like a park's visitor information, opens in your browser, and that website's own policy applies. If you share a trip's track from the app, it goes wherever you send it.

No tracking

There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no accounts. I don't sell or share your information. The one server of mine is Identify's, and it keeps only what the section on Identify's server says.

This website

theslowroad.app is hosted on GitHub Pages. The site uses no cookies, no scripts, no analytics and no outside fonts. GitHub logs the IP address of every visitor for security purposes, as GitHub's documentation says, and the GitHub Privacy Statement covers it.

Changes
